Gendered Positioning in Education Research Teams

  • From participation to relational inequality
    • Women are strongly represented in education research, but numerical participation does not necessarily imply equivalent academic practices (Abramo et al., 2021; Silander et al., 2013; Hanasono et al., 2019).
  • Research teams as directed and hierarchical relations
    • Research team formation is a hierarchical process rather than a relationship between formally equivalent actors: PIs often convene colleagues to form a research team for a project
  • Gender as a relational mechanism
    • Gender may shape team formation through three distinct mechanisms: who initiates nominations as PI (ego), who is incorporated as COI (alter), and whether ties are more likely between researchers of the same gender (homophily).

Institutional Positioning in Education Research Teams

  • Institutional similarity as structured opportunity
    • Similarity in institutional prestige, sector, or territorial profile may reflect active selection.
    • But it may also arise from shared social foci, such as academic circuits and organisations that structure opportunities for contact (Feld, 1981; McPherson et al., 2001).
  • Gendered returns to institutional position
    • Institutional prestige provides unequal resources and structure boundaries within the scientific field.
    • We examine whether this institutional effects is associated with different relational returns for women and men.

Research Gap and Contribution

  • What remains unexplored?
    • Research on gender inequality in science has primarily examined productivity, grant success, and career outcomes, offering less insight into how gender inequality is relationally organised (Huang et al., 2020; Nielsen, 2016).
    • Institutional effects leave open whether women and men obtain the same relational returns when forming research team networks as PIs.
  • Empirical focus
    • We model directed PI-to-COI relations in education research projects.
  • Contribution
    • We conceptualise gender inequality in funded research teams as an institutionally conditioned relational process, and test whether institutional prestige similarity operates differently for female and male PIs.

Research Hypotheses

  • Gender and team formation

    • Hypothesis 1: Female researchers show higher team-formation activity as PIs than male researchers.
    • Hypothesis 2: Female researchers are less likely than male researchers to be selected as coinvestigators (COIs).
    • Hypothesis 3: PIs are more likely to select COIs of the same gender.

  • Institutional assortativity

    • Hypothesis 4: Research-team ties are more likely between researchers affiliated with institutions of similar prestige.

  • Gendered relational returns to prestige as PI

    • Hypothesis 5: Female PIs will show a stronger tendency than male PIs to select co-investigators from institutions of similar prestige to their own.

Research Design

  • Data source and scope
    Analysis uses data on FONDECYT research teams that were awarded funding. This means that the network excludes research teams that applied but were not funded.

  • Directed network structure
    The network is modelled as a directed graph: PIs nominate co-investigators.

  • Temporal definition of ties
    Collaboration ties are retained for the full duration of each funded project, according to the number of years specified in the grant.

  • Analytical sample
    The longitudinal network includes 744 researchers observed across 9 waves from 2017 to 2025.

  • Analytical strategy
    Stochastic Actor-Oriented Models (SAOM): to analyse the evolution of longitudinal research project networks.

Data

  • Source (a): ANID funding registry (2016-2025)
    Full record of awarded research instruments from the ANID’S Chilean Ministry of Science.

  • Source (b): Team rosters of awarded projects
    Full record of research team members in educational research, both: early childhood, primary & secondary education, and higher education.

  • Source (c): Institutional characteristics
    University sector, institutional prestige (accreditation years), and institutional territorial profile were drawn from the Chilean Ministry of Education’s open-data enrolment registry for higher education.

Variables: Node covariates

  • Female
    Binary indicator of sex assigned at birth from administrative records (0 = male; 1 = female).

  • Institutional prestige
    Number of accreditation years awarded by the Chilean Commission for Quality Assurance (research, teaching, institutional management, internal quality assurance, and community engagement).

  • University sector
    Dummy indicator: 1 = private institution; 0 = public institution.

  • Institutional territorial profile
    Binary indicator: 1 = primarily metropolitan, 0 = primarily regional.

Variables: Dyadic covariates

  • Same institution
    Both researchers are affiliated with the same institution.

  • Prior COI–COI coparticipation
    Both researchers previously co-appeared as co-investigators on an awarded research project.

  • Same Education evaluation panel
    Both researchers are part of a research project in the same grant assessment panel (early childhood, primary & secondary education vs higher education).

Discussion

  • From participation to gendered network returns

    • Negative female-ego and non-significant female-alter effects shift attention from participation to team formation under female leadership.
    • Stronger prestige matching among female PIs is consistent with gender-differentiated network returns.

  • Assortativity as embedded opportunity
    Prestige, sectoral, and territorial similarity, alongside structural effects, may suggest that team formation reflects embedded, institutional opportunity structures.

  • PI-to-CoI formation as a policy site

    • As PI-to-CoI ties are shaped by gendered organisational processes, funding agencies should monitor research teams and support cross-boundary collaboration.

Limitations

  • Current limitations
    • Gender conceived as a binary variable.
    • The analysis also focuses only on awarded grants, rather than the full population of submitted proposals, including both successful and unsuccessful applications.
  • Modelling eligibility and career trajectories
    • Current model specification assumes that all researchers appearing in the data are nominable throughout the 2017 to 2025 period.
    • This simplifies more complex career dynamics, such as doctoral entry, research drop out, death, international migration, or withdrawal from the FONDECYT research circuit.
